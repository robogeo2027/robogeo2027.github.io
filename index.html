Abstract

Multi-camera bird’s-eye-view (BEV) perception models are commonly trained and evaluated on a fixed camera rig. However, real deployment may involve camera-mounting changes on the same vehicle or transfer to different vehicle platforms. Existing evaluations rely on proxies rather than reproducing such camera-geometry changes. Extrinsic-only perturbation leaves the input images unchanged, whereas cross-domain generalization entangles camera geometry with domain shift. We introduce RoboGeo, a CARLA-based benchmark for camera-geometry robustness in multi-camera BEV perception. RoboGeo re-renders the same scenes under changed camera geometry, producing geometrically consistent image–extrinsic pairs. We define three diagnostic conditions that separate extrinsic-only perturbation, viewpoint change with base extrinsics, and viewpoint change with the corresponding extrinsics. RoboGeo provides two protocols, viewpoint perturbation and cross-platform transfer to the SUV and Bus layouts. Robustness under each is quantified by the mean Viewpoint Robustness Score (mVRS) and the Cross-Platform Transfer Score (CTS), respectively. We benchmark representative BEV segmentation and 3D object detection models, providing insights into the relationship between view transformation and camera-geometry robustness.

Key Findings

1

Extrinsic-only perturbation is a poor proxy

Perturbing only the extrinsics (EXT) leaves the input images unchanged and underestimates the degradation from a real viewpoint change. Under changed images (IMG), every benchmarked model degrades — even the most robust model in each task loses more than 13% of its unperturbed performance.

2

View transformation drives robustness

Robustness varies systematically with how a model maps image features into BEV space. Backward-projection models transfer most robustly across platforms, recovering up to 76.9% (detection) and 67.5% (segmentation) of the oracle on the SUV.

3

Cross-platform transfer is far from solved

The Bus differs from the Sedan the most in camera height, spacing, and orientation. Even with the correct target extrinsics supplied (CAL), the best models reach only 48.1% (detection) and 36.8% (segmentation) of the oracle, with most entries below half.

4

Existing methods narrow but do not close the gap

Extrinsic augmentation changes the calibration but never the images: it adds little robustness and can lower clean performance. Cross-domain methods help more — PD-BEV lifts BEVDepth’s Bus transfer from 16.6% to 41.8% CTS — yet no method closes the gap on either protocol. RoboGeo remains far from saturated.
