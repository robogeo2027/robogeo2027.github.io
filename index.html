Abstract

Multi-camera bird’s-eye-view (BEV) perception models are commonly trained and evaluated on a fixed camera rig. However, real deployment may involve camera-mounting changes on the same vehicle or transfer to different vehicle platforms. Existing evaluations rely on proxies rather than reproducing such camera-geometry changes. Extrinsic-only perturbation leaves the input images unchanged, whereas cross-domain generalization entangles camera geometry with domain shift. We introduce RoboGeo, a CARLA-based benchmark for camera-geometry robustness in multi-camera BEV perception. RoboGeo re-renders the same scenes under changed camera geometry, producing geometrically consistent image–extrinsic pairs. We define three diagnostic conditions that separate extrinsic-only perturbation, viewpoint change with base extrinsics, and viewpoint change with the corresponding extrinsics. RoboGeo provides two protocols, viewpoint perturbation and cross-platform transfer to the SUV and Bus layouts. Robustness under each is quantified by the mean Viewpoint Robustness Score (mVRS) and the Cross-Platform Transfer Score (CTS), respectively. We benchmark representative BEV segmentation and 3D object detection models, providing insights into the relationship between view transformation and camera-geometry robustness.

Viewpoint Perturbation

This protocol rotates cameras of the base rig about roll, pitch, or yaw to model camera-rig changes such as vibration or mount shift. Drag the video — horizontally for yaw, vertically for pitch — or use the dials (one axis at a time, ±20° in 4° steps; front camera shown). All pose videos share a single timeline: the scene never stops, only the camera geometry changes.

Key Findings

1

Extrinsic-only perturbation is a poor proxy

Perturbing only the extrinsics (EXT) leaves the input images unchanged and underestimates the degradation from a real viewpoint change. Under changed images (IMG), every benchmarked model degrades — even the most robust model in each task loses more than 13% of its unperturbed performance.

2

View transformation drives robustness

Robustness varies systematically with how a model maps image features into BEV space. Backward-projection models transfer most robustly across platforms, recovering up to 76.9% (detection) and 67.5% (segmentation) of the oracle on the SUV.

3

Cross-platform transfer is far from solved

The Bus differs from the Sedan the most in camera height, spacing, and orientation. Even with the correct target extrinsics supplied (CAL), the best models reach only 48.1% (detection) and 36.8% (segmentation) of the oracle, with most entries below half.

4

Existing methods narrow but do not close the gap

Extrinsic augmentation changes the calibration but never the images: it adds little robustness and can lower clean performance. Cross-domain methods help more — PD-BEV lifts BEVDepth’s Bus transfer from 16.6% to 41.8% CTS — yet no method closes the gap on either protocol. RoboGeo remains far from saturated.

Dataset

Every camera geometry is rendered over the same underlying scenes, so images and extrinsics stay geometrically consistent across the whole benchmark. Annotations follow the nuScenes schema, extended with object attributes and a four-level visibility computed per platform.

Scenes & frames

  • 268 driving scenes — 220 train / 48 held-out eval
  • Rendered at 5 Hz for all three platforms
  • 17,380 train + 3,792 eval frames per platform
  • 6 surround cameras per frame, fixed intrinsics

Camera geometries

  • 212 changed geometries beyond the base rig
  • Viewpoint perturbation: 210 — roll / pitch / yaw at ±4°, ±8°, ±12°, ±16°, ±20°
  • Per-camera (6 × 30) and all-camera (30) modes
  • Cross-platform: 2 — SUV and Bus layouts

Structure

RoboGeo/
├── viewpoint_perturbation/                    # rotated rigs, one tar per camera and angle
│   ├── RGB-CAM_FRONT_yaw8pitch0roll0.tar      #   6 cameras x 31 settings = 186 tars
│   ├── RGB-CAM_BACK_LEFT_yaw0pitch0roll-20.tar
│   └── viewpoint_metadata.json
├── platform_layouts/                          # one tar per camera and platform
│   ├── RGB-CAM_FRONT.tar                      #   Sedan (base rig, no prefix)
│   ├── RGB-suv-CAM_FRONT.tar                  #   SUV
│   └── RGB-bus-CAM_FRONT.tar                  #   Bus
└── metadata/                                  # nuScenes-schema annotations,
    └── carla_geobev_meta.tar.zst              #   extrinsics, attributes, visibility

Perturbation tars are named RGB-<CAMERA>_yaw<Y>pitch<P>roll<R>.tar with angles in degrees, so RGB-CAM_FRONT_yaw8pitch0roll0.tar is the front camera rotated by yaw +8°. Cameras use the nuScenes names (CAM_FRONT, CAM_FRONT_LEFT, CAM_FRONT_RIGHT, CAM_BACK, CAM_BACK_LEFT, CAM_BACK_RIGHT), and each has 31 settings: 30 single-axis rotations plus the unperturbed yaw0pitch0roll0. Because renders are stored per camera, both the per-camera and the all-camera mode are assembled from the same tars: rotate one camera and read the other five unperturbed, or take the same angle for all six. The Sedan base rig in platform_layouts/ provides the NORMAL condition and the training data for both protocols.

Getting started

The dataset is gated — request access on the dataset page first, then authenticate with huggingface_hub. The full release is large, so download only the geometries you need with allow_patterns. 

from huggingface_hub import snapshot_download

# One perturbation setting: front camera, yaw +8 degrees
snapshot_download(
    "shdragron/RoboGeo",
    repo_type="dataset",
    allow_patterns=[
        "viewpoint_perturbation/RGB-CAM_FRONT_yaw8pitch0roll0.tar",
        "viewpoint_perturbation/viewpoint_metadata.json",
        "metadata/*",
    ],
    local_dir="RoboGeo",
)

# Or a whole platform layout: every camera of the Bus
snapshot_download(
    "shdragron/RoboGeo",
    repo_type="dataset",
    allow_patterns=["platform_layouts/RGB-bus-*.tar", "metadata/*"],
    local_dir="RoboGeo",
)
